# Finding Expression

Find expressions that both improve the writing and help Braden recognize what
he is reaching for. Take initiative: explore worthwhile possibilities, choose
which to show, and synthesize what his reactions reveal. New wording can be
right even if he would not have independently arrived at those words.

Use [writing-voice](../../writing-voice/SKILL.md) for conversational precision
with a poetic turn. When a destination workflow is already active, it continues
to own saving, source preservation, selection, and the finished artifact.

## Make The Possibility Tangible

Put actual prose in front of him rather than asking him to diagnose a style
preference before seeing an attempt. Prefer blockquotes for the passages shown
in chat, with labels and commentary outside, so the writing can be read on its
own and the user can select one version or parts of several. Give the thought the length it needs:
meaningful details, examples, turns, and emphasis are development, not excess
merely because they take several paragraphs. If a meaningful part is hard to
connect, explore the connection rather than silently dropping it.

Choose the next surface by what would help his judgment. One strong expression
may settle it; a contrast may reveal different centers; a gradation may locate
the right degree of directness, humor, or poetic intensity. These are techniques,
not a fixed count, taxonomy, or mandatory menu. Explore beyond the first workable
wording, but show only possibilities that expose a meaningful difference or
make a promising improvement tangible.

Recognize wording that already works; exploration can develop what follows it
or establish that it should stay. Work at the scale of the live possibility:
a sentence, paragraph, opening, ending, or whole piece. Keep settled material stable while a local choice is
being explored. When the proposed move changes the whole, show enough of the
whole to make its consequence judgeable. Do not make the user compare complete
rewrites to decide one sentence.

Keep commentary proportionate to the choice; that economy does not limit the
proposed writing's length. Recommend the strongest direction when useful and
explain what the difference buys. The user should not have to assemble fragments
or do the synthesis. The destination workflow owns the response format when
these proposals accompany saved work; standalone exploration uses the smallest
readable form that makes the choice tangible.

## Let Recognition Change The Next Attempt

A reaction teaches more than which option won. It may recognize one version's
rhythm and another's attention, reject the premise shared by both, or reveal
that the whole thought is elsewhere. Carry that understanding into the next
attempt; combining parts is useful only when the resulting passage holds together.
Apply a reaction at the scope it illuminates; a preference about one passage
is not a universal style rule.

When wording keeps feeling wrong, reconsider what the expression is trying to
do before polishing it again. A focused question can resolve an ambiguity that
prevents a useful attempt; otherwise let a concrete passage test understanding.
Exploring a possibility does not itself authorize replacing a saved expression.
Follow the destination workflow and the latitude of the user's request.

Preserve the force and particulars of the thought. Lived experience can be
present in attention, humor, surprise, and emphasis without adding an anecdote
or psychological explanation. Do not invent motives or facts to make a passage
deeper. If accuracy requires a substantive qualification, make that issue
visible rather than either hiding it or weakening every sentence.

Finding words can change the underlying thought; do not require a separate mode
switch to follow that discovery. Follow existing authorization for saving,
sending, or publishing; a concrete proposal does not require permission merely
to write it.
