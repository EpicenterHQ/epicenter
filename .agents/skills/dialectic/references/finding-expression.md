# Finding Expression

Find expressions that both improve the writing and help Braden recognize what
he is reaching for. Take initiative: explore worthwhile possibilities, choose
which to show, and synthesize what his reactions reveal. New wording can be
right even if he would not have independently arrived at those words.

Use [writing-voice](../../writing-voice/SKILL.md) for conversational precision
with a poetic turn. When a destination workflow is already active, it continues
to own saving, source preservation, selection, and response format. Existing
authorization governs changes to saved work; proposing wording needs no extra
permission and does not itself authorize replacing a saved expression.

## Make The Possibility Tangible

Show what a writing choice would produce: actual prose the author can read
and react to, rather than editing instructions or a description of its style.
A short introduction, simple labels, and quoted passages can help him enter
and compare the writing; let the live choice determine its presentation.
Give the thought the length it needs:
meaningful details, examples, turns, and emphasis are development, not excess
merely because they take several paragraphs. If a meaningful part is hard to
connect, explore the connection rather than silently dropping it.

Meaningfully different versions of the same material can reveal what the
author cares about before either of you can name it. That can help on the first
attempt or while developing a promising version. Show whole drafts when their
movement and endings determine the difference, and passages when the choice is
local. Keep settled material stable around a local exploration; wording that
already works may need to stay. One strong expression may settle it; show
additional possibilities for the difference or improvement they reveal, not
to fill a set.

Keep commentary proportionate to the choice; that economy does not limit the
proposed writing's length. Recommend the strongest direction when useful,
explain what it buys, and carry the synthesis so the user need not assemble
fragments to experience the result.

## Let Recognition Change The Next Attempt

A reaction teaches more than which option won. It may recognize one version's
rhythm and another's attention, reject the premise shared by both, or reveal
that the whole thought is elsewhere. Carry that understanding into the next
attempt; combining parts is useful only when the resulting passage holds together.
Apply a reaction at the scope it illuminates; a preference about one passage
is not a universal style rule.

Leave room for that reaction without directing it into a choice between your
interpretations. The passage can stand as the turn's contribution. Ask a
question when it helps you understand something unresolved, not merely to
solicit a verdict after every draft.

When wording keeps feeling wrong, reconsider what the expression is trying to
do before polishing it again. A focused question can resolve an ambiguity that
prevents a useful attempt; otherwise let a concrete passage test understanding.

Preserve the force and particulars of the thought. Lived experience can be
present in attention, humor, surprise, and emphasis without adding an anecdote
or psychological explanation. Do not invent motives or facts to make a passage
deeper. If accuracy requires a substantive qualification, make that issue
visible rather than either hiding it or weakening every sentence.

Finding words can change the underlying thought; do not require a separate mode
switch to follow that discovery.
