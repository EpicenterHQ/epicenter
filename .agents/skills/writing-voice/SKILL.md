# Writing Voice

Help Braden bring forward the fullest readable expression of what he is trying
to say. Judge the result by whether he can recognize it as “unmistakably me,”
in both its judgment and its sound. Write in
language Braden could say to an articulate friend: clear, natural, and precise,
with room for rhythm, wit, and surprise. Find phrasing that makes the thought
felt as well as understood. Use this craft throughout drafting and revision;
voice, structure, and meaning develop together.

## Find The Expression

Conversational precision with a poetic turn is an ambition, not a formula for
every paragraph. A turn earns its place by revealing a connection in the material.
Try a sharper verb, an unexpected comparison, a changed rhythm, or a plainer
sentence when it better carries the thought. Do not invent a motive, feeling,
or event to give the prose depth, or substitute ornament for the particulars.
Avoid explanations of what a thought signifies merely to make the paragraph
sound complete; a polished summary can add no meaning while erasing its force.

Build from the author's actual phrases, not just a summary of their meaning.
Keep the comparisons, ordinary words, asides, and emphatic turns that make the
speaker recognizable; arrange and connect them before replacing them with
equally accurate paraphrases. Roughness can carry emotion, humor, tension,
uncertainty, or rhythm. “And then there's just a lot of Asian food” can earn its
own paragraph without becoming an explanation of everyday cultural connection.
Repair transcription errors and obstructive phrasing without treating every
“actually,” “of course,” or repetition as noise. Neither roughness nor polish
is a virtue by itself.

Choose the register from the piece's purpose and Braden's reactions. Preserve
substantive qualifications while removing hesitation that belongs only to the
act of dictation. Preserve the degree of conviction too: changing “I would
argue” into “I wonder whether” can weaken the thought even when its subject
stays the same. Do not strengthen real uncertainty into certainty either.
A direct argument can present a position for examination
without turning it into his personal belief. Personal writing can remain
unresolved without narrating every step of the conversation that produced it.

## Hear The Whole Passage

Writing for the ear is part of developing the piece. Listen for how the words
carry the thought, giving that attention more weight when the work will be
spoken aloud. An “ear pass” names this editorial focus, not a separate workflow.
The active writing workflow, such as page-writing or journal-writing, continues
to own the conversation and edits.

Read connected passages at speaking speed and judge each sentence with what
surrounds it. The four ear tests below guide attention, not a report to fill out.
Hear what the writing is doing, then judge it by what this piece needs.

When a reaction reveals a mismatch, rehear the passage under that correction
and revise at the scope it illuminates. Use the destination's exploration
workflow when contrasting expressions would help; an authorized direct edit
needs no options menu. Reread the changed passage in context to hear whether
its movement and the author's posture still belong together. After a whole-piece
pass, check that the opening, strongest concrete detail, and ending still serve
the same thought.

For work intended to be heard, read [spoken destinations](references/spoken-destinations.md)
for the extra judgments a listener's experience requires. Ordinary editing
does not impose a narration or social-video standard on every piece.

### Economy

Identify what each sentence or paragraph contributes: a claim, example, turn,
qualification, emphasis, or the experience of discovering the thought. Make that
contribution legible through arrangement, connection, and phrasing before
considering a cut. Repeated information may let the author return to a thought,
build pressure, or give it breathing room; new information is not its admission
test. Repair transcription noise and mechanical duplication with no expressive
work left. When unsure, preserve the passage.

Optional compression that drops meaningful material or reduces its movement
requires an explicit request to tighten, shorten, cut, or otherwise condense,
or selection of a proposed cut. A voice or ear pass does not by itself authorize
it, even after the first draft. For pages and journals, the destination's
authorship guidance owns that permission boundary. Keep the first draft faithful
and readable; do not preempt the author's later choice to whittle it down.

### Rhythm

Listen for long clauses, stacked abstractions, repeated openings, awkward
mouth-feel, false starts, and phrasing whose predictability drains its force.
Hear where a breath naturally belongs and where emphasis lands. Split a
sentence when it helps the listener follow, not merely to create short lines.
A pause or repetition may build pressure, preserve uncertainty, or make a joke
land. Judge the words before trying to fix the delivery or timing.

### Movement and progressive disclosure

Trace what the reader or listener encounters in order and what each turn needs.
An argument may explain a mechanism, a scene may accumulate particulars, and a
question may stay open. An early conclusion can orient the reader; a later one
can let the example earn its force. Choose the order this expression needs
without imposing suspense, a moral, or a changed meaning at the end.

### Human recognition

“Unmistakably me” is a test of the resulting passage, not a label the agent can
declare satisfied. Hear whether its words, characteristic connections, concrete
details, humor, uncertainty, conviction, and movement belong to Braden. Retaining
the facts alone does not establish recognition. Let his reactions show which
phrases and turns he recognizes, and carry those forward in the next attempt.

New language can pass this test when he recognizes the thought and how it moves,
even if he would not have arrived at that exact sentence alone. Preserve room for
that discovery without treating smoother or more polished prose as inherently
better. Remove polish that makes the passage more solemn, certain, universal,
or professionally scripted than its intended expression. Recognition matters
more than grammatical perfection; the person should remain audible.

## Respect The Form

A message, essay, button, and technical explanation have different jobs. Let
those jobs shape length and register while keeping the person audible. Clarity
does not require corporate scaffolding or false authority.

Use punctuation to make movement legible. Prefer periods. Use colons for a real
explanation, semicolons for closely related independent clauses, and parentheses
for genuine asides. Avoid en dashes; default away from em dashes except for a
real interruption or high-emphasis aside.
