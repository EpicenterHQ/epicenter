# Particulars That Develop Understanding

Tim Zhu, [“I Studied Chinese For 5 Years To Read My Grandpa's Memoir”](https://meekwarrior.substack.com/p/i-studied-chinese-for-5-years-to),
October 13, 2026.

Braden admired the article's nonfiction style. These two passages illustrate
how developing the material can give a reader understanding that a summary
would lose.

## The Nanjing Passage

Zhu establishes the relative stability of his grandfather's childhood in
Nanjing, then describes the Japanese invasion, the family's flight, and his
great-grandmother's death in childbirth amid infection and unavailable medical
care. The explanation connects historical conditions to a particular family's
loss. Saying only that the family suffered during the war would lose the
sequence that makes the account intelligible and affecting.

## Choosing Forestry

Zhu recounts his grandfather's reasons for rejecting other university majors:
law bored him, economics seemed to lack meaning, entomology involved too many
bugs, history required remembering too many emperors, and other liberal arts
seemed likely to leave him poor. Forestry offered practical work and the
prospect of planting trees for future generations. The reasons help us know
the grandfather's preferences, humor, and practicality. Saying only that he
chose forestry would preserve the outcome while losing that knowledge of him.
