The steering wheel is a perfect example of an interface designed at the right
level of abstraction.

There are many other interfaces we could have chose. A car could use a lever
that you push left or right. It could use a joystick. It could even use a laser
pointer that tells the car where to go, or a collection of random buttons.

Any of those interfaces could steer a car. What makes the steering wheel so
good is that it turns the driver's intention into one continuous, intuitive
motion: left or right, a little or a lot. You just turn the wheel.

A perfect abstraction exposes exactly what the user needs and hides the rest
of the machinery underneath.
The steering wheel preserves the driver's simple motion while hiding the
hydraulics, gears, and every hinge connecting the wheel in their hands to the
wheels on the road. That balance is what makes it smart, intuitive, and
tasteful.
